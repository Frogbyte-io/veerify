What's confusing today

Settings live in 5 places/settings#tools (team), product Features tab, product Feedback/Categories/Statuses/GitHub tabs, /marketing/settings (with its own product picker), /support/settings (per inbox). Roadmap and Changelog have no settings page at all.
Two switches per toolA tool shows for a product only when the team toggle and the product toggle are both on. Roadmap, Changelog and Marketing default to off at both levels, so turning one on in Settings → Tools changes nothing for any product.
Tools that are off disappearDisabled modules are removed from the sidebar, so people can't find them. Members get no "ask an admin" path, and admins get no "enable" path from where they are working.
Three role systems, none shown up frontWorkspace owner/admin/member, team admin/member, and inbox agent/supervisor/admin. You only learn you lack a permission from a disabled switch or a read-only banner once you're on the page.
The current scope is unclearThe team switcher mixes organizations and teams (the "Default" team shows as the workspace name or "All projects"). Marketing has its own product picker, Feedback has a filter, and Support is scoped to the team.
Personal and admin settings are mixed/settings puts Profile/Security/Appearance next to Workspace/Teams/Tools. The sidebar's "Members" and "Notifications" links go to hash tabs in the same page.

Proposed principles (apply to every variation)

  1. Always show the scope. A breadcrumb Workspace › Team › Product on every page, and one switcher (no second picker inside Marketing).
  2. One switch per tool. Tools are turned on per product. The team level is derived ("on for 2 of 3 products"), so the team toggle goes away. Support is the exception: it is team-scoped, and creating an inbox turns it on.
  3. Each tool has its settings in the same place. Every tool gets a Settings tab in the same spot (Feedback → categories, statuses, GitHub; Marketing → site, keys; Support → inbox, SLA, automations). Product settings keep only product-wide items: name, visibility, domain, appearance, embed, danger.
  4. Never hide without a way back. Tools that are off show greyed under "More tools", with Enable for admins and Request for members.
  5. Show permissions before people try. A lock line under anything they can't change: "Team admins manage this · Ask Kari".
  6. Separate personal from admin settings. The avatar menu holds Profile, Security, Notifications and Appearance. A separate "Workspace settings" holds Members, Teams, Tools, Billing and Integrations.

Variations

The product is the main context, similar to how Vercel or Linear handle projects. Pick a product in the sidebar and see only the tools that are on for it. Tools scoped to the team (Support) sit in their own section below.

Recommendation

Use A (product-first) as the main structure and add C's Tools matrix as a page in Workspace settings. A answers "where are the settings?" (product, then tool, then Settings). The matrix answers "what is on, and who controls it?" in one place. Take B's "All products" option for triage across products.

Suggested order: ① remove the team-level toggle (make it derived) and add a Settings tab to each tool; ② split personal and workspace settings and add lock lines with "Request"; ③ add the product switcher and breadcrumb, and remove the Marketing picker; ④ build the Tools matrix and the guided "Add tool" flow.
